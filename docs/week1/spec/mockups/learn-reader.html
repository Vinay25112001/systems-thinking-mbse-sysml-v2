Chapter 4 · Boundaries, context and operational environments

4.1 A boundary is chosen for a question

A system of interest is chosen for a question. Consider “Will adding aircraft reduce average passenger waiting time?” and three candidate boundaries for AeroLink. A boundary around the aircraft cannot answer it, because waiting time is not a property of an aircraft. A boundary around flight operations can count flights, but if charger and pad turnaround is the real limit it will predict that more aircraft always help: the cause of waiting lies outside it.

A boundary around the air-taxi service (fleet, operations center, booking and dispatch, the vertiport pads and chargers, and passenger demand) contains the feedback between demand, the charging queue and waiting time, and is still small enough to analyze. SEBoK puts the general point this way: observers allocate boundaries that reflect their own needs, so several systems of interest can be identified in one situation, and each must include a set of elements that represents a system whole.1

Check your understanding (optional, not graded)
Common error. “The biggest boundary is the safest.” A boundary that includes everything cannot be analyzed; choose the smallest boundary that still contains the causes of what you are asking about.
