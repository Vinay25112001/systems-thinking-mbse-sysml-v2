## Engineering method

1. **Technology readiness assessment.** Choose the enabling technologies your requirements depend on. For each: what it does for the enterprise, its benefits and limitations, a TRL read against the scale wording (the environment words decide it), and its basis: cited evidence, or a labeled assumption in the register with an owner and a validation action. Mark each critical or not, link the requirements and capabilities it affects, and give every critical technology below TRL 6 a maturity risk. Lead the summary with the lowest critical TRL, and say what the TRL does not tell you (integration, producibility). Hard gates: a name and a description of what the technology does for the enterprise; a TRL from 1 to 9; a basis: cited evidence, or a labeled assumption with an owner and a validation action; at least one requirement or capability it affects that exists in the record; a linked maturity risk when the technology is critical and below TRL 6. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 5 ‘What a readiness assessment is for’; Session 1, slide 6 ‘The scale, read exactly, and what it does not tell you’; Session 1, slide 7 ‘Maturity in practice: hype, the s-curve and shared meanings’; Session 1, slide 8 ‘Evidence, or a labeled assumption’; Session 1, slide 9 ‘Worked example I: AeroLink’s three enabling technologies’.)*
2. **Risk statements and ratings.** Hold a risk workshop (each member brings two risks). Write each risk as condition (what is known today), “if” (the uncertain event) and “then” (the consequences, naming what is harmed), with no mitigation in the statement. Rate likelihood and consequence on the course scales and write the reason for each rating. Rank by the matrix level and then by consequence; never multiply the two ordinal numbers as if they were quantities. Include at least 1 security risk (an attack, misuse or fault that causes loss of an asset: material resources and infrastructure; system capability; human resources; intellectual property; data and information; derivative non-tangible (image, reputation, trust)) and at least 1 resilience risk (an adversity that degrades the delivered capability). *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 10 ‘The risk process: five activities and what each produces’; Session 1, slide 11 ‘Writing a risk: condition, if, then’; Session 1, slide 12 ‘Rating risks: defined scales and the reporting matrix’; Session 1, slide 13 ‘What the matrix cannot say: ordinal scales are not numbers’; Session 1, slide 15 ‘Security and resilience risks belong in the same register’.)*
3. **Handling, owner, trigger and monitoring.** Evaluate all the handling options (avoid, control (mitigate), transfer, accept (assume), watch) before choosing one; record the owner, the treatment (unless the risk is accepted or watched) and the trigger or watch item that tells the owner to act. For a security or resilience risk, say whether the treatment helps the enterprise avoid (eliminate or reduce exposure to stress), withstand (resist capability degradation when stressed), recover (replenish lost capability after degradation). Hard gates for every risk: condition, “if” and “then” parts, in that order; an uncertain future event, not something that has already happened (that is an issue); no mitigation written into the statement; likelihood and consequence on the 1–5 scales, each with a rationale; a handling option, an owner and a trigger (and a treatment unless accepted or watched); at least one requirement, capability or scenario it affects that exists in the record. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 14 ‘Handling options: evaluate all four before choosing’; Session 1, slide 16 ‘Worked example II: writing, rating and handling two risks’; Session 1, slide 17 ‘Monitoring, triggers and the pitfalls to avoid’.)*
4. **The register, linked.** Keep the assumptions, risks, opportunities and unknowns distinct and actionable: every assumption with an owner and a validation action, at least 1 opportunity with its action, and every Week 2 unknown (TBD/TBR) still in the register until it is closed. Link each entry to the requirements, capabilities, scenarios, technologies or TPMs it affects. Anything that has already happened goes in the issue log, kept apart from the risks. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 4 ‘Five kinds of entry: assumption, risk, opportunity, unknown, issue’; Session 1, slide 18 ‘Worked example III: the register, linked to the package’.)*
5. **Technical performance measures.** For the riskiest requirements, define TPMs: the requirement tracked, the measure of performance it derives from, unit and sense, threshold, objective, current best estimate, contingency, and the basis of the estimate. The worst case is the estimate moved by the contingency toward the threshold; the margin is the threshold minus the worst case. Plan a value at two or more future review points and link the risk the TPM watches; a TPM whose worst case is already outside the threshold must link a risk, or an issue if the breach is certain (a TPM breach is a risk). Hard gates: a measured requirement that exists, and the measure (MOP) it derives from; threshold, objective, current best estimate and contingency as numbers, with a unit and a sense; an objective on the good side of the threshold; a basis for the current estimate; a planned value at two or more future review points; a linked risk (or issue) in the register when the worst case is outside the threshold: a TPM breach is a risk. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 19 ‘From MOE and MOP to TPM’; Session 1, slide 20 ‘Current estimate, contingency, worst case and margin’; Session 1, slide 21 ‘Worked example IV: AeroLink’s TPMs, margins and planned profile’; Session 1, slide 22 ‘One chain through all three instruments’.)*
6. **Frame the trade study.** Start from the approved decision statement, traced to a requirement or objective in your record. List at least 3 genuinely different alternatives including the baseline (status quo). Choose at least 4 criteria, each traced to a measure of effectiveness (or an objective), non-overlapping, with cost and risk handled explicitly. Treat security as a must-meet screen where a minimum expectation applies, and treat any requirement the decision must meet (such as a capacity requirement) the same way; include resilience as a criterion where the decision changes how the enterprise withstands or recovers from a disruption. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 3 ‘What decision management is for’; Session 2, slide 4 ‘Framing the decision, objectives and measures’; Session 2, slide 8 ‘Worked example I: the frame and the capacities’; Session 2, slide 9 ‘Worked example II: criteria, screens and weights’.)*
7. **Value functions, swing weights, scores and computation.** For each criterion set a walk-away point (value 0) and a stretch goal (value 1) and document the value function. Elicit swing weights: rank the swings from walk-away to stretch, give the most valuable swing 100 points and the others points relative to it, write the rationale naming the range, and normalize the points to weights that sum to one. Score every alternative on every criterion with its source, convert scores to values and compute the additive value; keep the calculation reproducible. An alternative that fails a screen, or scores beyond a walk-away point on any criterion, is walked away from: it is not ranked, and the tool leaves it out of the sensitivity. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 5 ‘Value functions: from measure space to value space’; Session 2, slide 6 ‘Swing weights: importance and range’; Session 2, slide 7 ‘Alternatives and the additive value model’; Session 2, slide 10 ‘Worked example III: scores, values and totals’.)*
8. **Sensitivity, recommendation and derived requirements.** Vary each weight with the others rescaled in proportion and find where the top rank changes among the alternatives that pass every screen (a reversal to a screened-out alternative is no reversal); state the reversal point of the most heavily weighted criterion, with its number, and what it means. If you recommend an alternative that is not ranked first, give the reason in the conditions. Check the pitfalls: anchors fixed rather than taken from the alternatives, compensation hiding a walk-away score, double counting. Recommend an alternative that passes every screen and lies inside every walk-away point, with its conditions, its residual risk and what would change the decision, and list any derived requirements the decision creates. Hard gates: a decision statement that names the objective and traces to a requirement or objective in the record; the baseline (status quo) among the alternatives; alternatives that differ in at least one score; each criterion traced to an MOE (or an objective) in the record; each criterion with a walk-away point and a stretch goal that differ; swing points for every criterion, each with a rationale; a score for every alternative on every criterion; the reversal point of the most heavily weighted criterion stated; a recommendation that passes every screen and lies inside every walk-away point, with conditions, residual risk and what would change it. Advisories: no cost criterion: cost is usually a criterion or a stated constraint; one criterion carries more than half of the weight; two criteria trace to the same MOE (possible double counting); no resilience or security criterion or screen; the top rank reverses within ±0.05 of a base weight; an alternative scores beyond a walk-away point: record it as failing a screen (it is left out of the ranking and the sensitivity); the recommendation is not the top-ranked alternative: give the reason in the conditions; the sensitivity note states no number within ±0.02 of a computed reversal point. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 11 ‘Worked example IV: sensitivity and rank reversal’; Session 2, slide 12 ‘Weighted-sum pitfalls: normalization, compensation, double counting’; Session 2, slide 13 ‘Worked example V: the recommendation, with conditions’.)*

## The lab tool

The lab tool imports your team’s Week 2 model-inputs file and adds Parts 11–14. It carries your Week 2 unknowns and assumptions into the register, shows the TRL wording, the likelihood bands and the consequence anchors as you rate, and computes the worst case and margin of every TPM, the weights, values, totals and ranking of the trade study, and the reversal point of every criterion, with the same arithmetic the lecture uses. Each technology, risk, TPM and the trade study lists its hard gates, each marked pass or fails, and its advisories, labeled advisory, which never stop an entry counting; only entries that pass every hard gate count toward the minimums. One member can enter the record and export the JSON file; teammates import it to continue the same record. Everything is saved in your browser only, so export after every working session.
