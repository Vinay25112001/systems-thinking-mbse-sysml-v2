Week 11 — Physical / Interfaces. AeroLink is the instructor’s FICTIONAL worked example; this model is of your team’s own enterprise.

## Purpose

Model your team’s own physical architecture and interfaces, never AeroLink, in the SysML v2 model your team extended in Week 10. Every Week 4 physical element and Week 5 component becomes a part; every function is performed by the physical element that runs it; every logical element is allocated to the physical elements that realize it, function by function; every Week 5 interface becomes ports, an interface definition with the flows of what crosses, and an interface usage, or a connection for an organizational interface; every interface requirement is allocated to its interface and to the function that realizes it; and your first satisfy claims state, with their basis, which design element meets which requirement. Then review the model against your Week 5 record with the course checklist, give every finding a disposition, and run the model through the course-designated checker until it reports no errors. Model v0.4 is what Week 12 verifies.

## Engineering method

1. **Physical elements and components.** Each Week 4 physical element becomes a part usage with its ID inside physicalArchitecture, and each Week 5 component a part usage nested in its element. A part definition already in Physical with the same name is reused; link every other Week 8 or Week 9 part definition to the element it stands for, so the element’s definition specializes it and the requirements whose subject it is still conform, or record why it stays separate. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 5 ‘Layers: elements and their components’; Session 1, slide 8 ‘Physical elements and components as parts’.)*
2. **Functions on physical elements.** Each function is performed by the physical element Week 4 placed it on (course convention: at level 1; a component’s Week 5 functions are written into its documentation). Choose the physical element for every function your team added in Week 10. An element that performs nothing needs a disposition: a medium or an enabling element is a valid reason. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 9 ‘Functions performed by physical parts’.)*
3. **Logical-to-physical allocation.** The tool allocates each logical part to every physical part that performs one of its functions, with one nested allocation per function, so the allocation cannot contradict the placements. Compare the allocation matrix with your Week 4 allocation table and record any difference as a finding. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 10 ‘The logical-to-physical allocation, with nested allocations’; Session 1, slide 11 ‘AeroLink worked example I: the allocation matrix’.)*
4. **Ports and what crosses.** For every physical and information interface, set each crossing: the item (an information ID, or the name of an item in your model or added here) and its direction, from end A or to end A. Add an item where none exists: an information item takes the next free ID; energy, material and mechanical-load items are named. The tool writes one port definition per interface from end A’s point of view and types end B by its conjugate; where it cannot read a direction from your record it assumes end A to end B and asks you to decide. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 15 ‘Conjugation: one port definition, both ends’; Session 1, slide 18 ‘AeroLink worked example II: the charging interface’s ports’.)*
5. **Interfaces and connections.** The tool writes an interface definition with a flow per crossing and an interface usage with the IF ID for each physical or information interface, and a connection for each organizational interface; an external end gets its port on a reference part that subsets the external in the context, and an external your Week 4 or Week 5 record added is added to the context first. A crossing that names no item is left out until you name its item. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 4 ‘Interface definitions and interface usages’; Session 2, slide 7 ‘Information, physical and organizational interfaces in the model’.)*
6. **Interface requirements.** Each Week 5 interface requirement is allocated to its interface usage and to the function at one end that realizes it: confirm or change the functions the tool suggests. An interface requirement your Week 9 model does not have is written into Requirements, derived from its Week 5 parents, with the interface definition as its subject. Allocate any requirement an open item of Weeks 9 or 10 assigned to the physical architecture, or re-date it with its reason and owner. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 9 ‘Interface requirements in the model’; Session 2, slide 10 ‘Allocating interface requirements: the open items close’.)*
7. **Satisfy claims.** Write at least 2 satisfy claims (a team of three: 1): a requirement, the physical element or interface whose type conforms to its subject, the design value of the attribute it constrains where you have one, and the basis (an analysis, an estimate, a supplier figure). The tool leaves out a claim whose element does not conform and does not count a claim without a basis. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 11 ‘Satisfy claims: the design asserts it meets a requirement’.)*
8. **Review, audit and checker.** Read the model against the 8 checks of the course checklist; record for each whether the model meets it, with how you checked, or the finding and what you will do. Give every finding a disposition: an open item with an owner and a week, or why no change is needed; a disposition cannot claim a fix. Run model v0.4 through the course-designated checker until it reports no errors, and explain every warning in the log. A PASS does not show that connected ports match, that a flow runs the right way or that a satisfy claim is true. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 21 ‘What the checker checks about ports, and what it cannot’; Session 2, slide 15 ‘The interface review checklist’.)*

| Satisfy claim | A design value or analysis the team can defend | satisfy the requirement by the physical part (or interface) usage whose type conforms to its subject, with the design value as an attribute redefinition on that usage and the basis in the doc | Physical |

## The interface review checklist (course convention)

| Check | What the tool shows |
|---|---|
| Every Week 4 physical element and Week 5 component is a part in physicalArchitecture, with its Phase I ID, and every Week 8 or 9 part definition is reused, specialized or given a reason | The parts table lists each element, its definition and its components. |
| Every logical element is allocated to the physical elements that realize it, and every function it performs is allocated, nested, to the physical element that performs it | The allocation matrix is computed from the functions each element performs; trace findings list what is missing. |
| Every interface end is a port on the element Week 5 names, and the second end uses the conjugate, so every out item meets an in item | The tool writes one port definition per interface and its conjugate. The checker does not test that two connected ports match: it accepts an interface whose definition has a conjugate end connecting a port typed by the plain definition, and an interface between ports of unrelated definitions. Check each port’s own type. |
| Every crossing has a flow in the right direction, and the item is produced at the source end and consumed at the target end | Directions are computed from the producers and consumers where they can be; an assumed direction is a gap. The checker accepts a flow in the wrong direction, and a flow between two different items: check that both ends of a flow name the same item. |
| Every Week 5 interface is modeled (physical and information as interfaces, organizational as connections), and no interface is invented | The interface table lists each Week 5 interface with its usage and ports. |
| Every interface requirement is in the model, derived from its parent, and allocated to its interface and to the function that realizes it | Trace findings list interface requirements allocated to no function. |
| Every satisfy claim names a part whose type conforms to the requirement’s subject and states its basis, a design value or an analysis, to be verified in Week 12 | The tool leaves out a claim whose part does not conform, or whose design value misses a limit it can read from the requirement, and asks for the basis. A limit it cannot read is yours to compare. |
| Every external system the enterprise exchanges with has an interface, or a recorded reason why not | Trace findings list the externals with no interface. |

## The lab tool

The lab tool reads two files: your Week 10 model v0.3 (.sysml) and your Week 5 model inputs (.json). It refuses AeroLink files, files of another team, a Week 9 model that has no logical architecture, a Week 4 file in the Week 5 field and files whose packages are missing, each with the reason. You link the part definitions Weeks 8 and 9 put into Physical, place the functions Week 4 did not place, set the item and direction of every crossing, add items with IDs that continue after the highest your files have used and are never reused, choose the functions of each interface requirement, allocate open requirements to physical elements and write the satisfy claims. The tool writes model v0.4: it fills the Physical package (parts, components, performs, port and interface definitions with their flows, interfaces and connections, the logical-to-physical allocation, the requirement allocations, the satisfy claims), adds the interface requirements your model lacks to Requirements and the externals your record added to the context. It never renumbers: an ID in your Week 5 file that names a different element in your model is reported and left out until you correct the Week 5 record. It computes the audit, keeps the review checklist and shows a readiness list with the reason for every item still open. Its checks are course acceptance checks: whether the model is valid is decided by the course-designated checker. A model v0.4 exported from the tool can be imported again; the tool replaces what it generated, so keep hand edits in a separate file until the last export. Everything is saved in your browser only: export after every working session, and share the workspace file with teammates.
