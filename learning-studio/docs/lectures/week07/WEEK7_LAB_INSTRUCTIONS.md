## Engineering method

1. **Assemble the package from its sources.** Correct each week where its feedback asks, export it again from its own lab tool, and load all six exports into the package tool. Hard gates: all six weekly exports loaded (Weeks 1 to 6); the same enterprise in every file. Advisories: the team code differs between files: check that every file is your team’s latest export; the Week 1 file is version 1 or 2: it still imports, but export it again from the current Week 1 tool so the package carries objectives and relationships. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 4 ‘The package is a baseline: the reference point for every later change’; Session 1, slide 5 ‘What the package contains’; Session 1, slide 8 ‘One enterprise, one ID, one meaning’.)*
2. **Read the coverage and the findings.** Read the coverage of each trace layer (needs → stakeholder requirements; stakeholder → system requirements; system requirements → architecture; architecture → interfaces; roadmap → package) and every finding the audit lists: two enterprises, week not ready, stale export, id no longer defined, wording drifted, id collision, disposition no longer true, childless need, orphan requirement, stakeholder requirement not flowed down, system requirement not allocated, capability without a function, element without an interface, external without an interface, increment serves an unknown id, open item from the baseline review, open item dropped. Then read your needs against their requirements yourself: a link can exist and carry only part of a need, which the coverage count cannot see. The tool reads requirements from your Week 2 and Week 5 records: a requirement written in another week belongs in your Week 2 record, exported again. Identity rule for an ID defined in two weeks: Same ID, same element only if the names match (ignoring case and punctuation) or one name contains the other whole; otherwise two elements share the ID: a collision, which no rationale can accept. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 9 ‘Bidirectional traceability: no orphans, no childless parents’; Session 1, slide 10 ‘Well formed and right: requirement verification and validation’; Session 1, slide 11 ‘Coverage, findings and dispositions’.)*
3. **Fix at the source, or disposition.** Fix what you can where it was made: correct the week’s record in its own lab tool, export it again and import the new file; a corrected finding disappears, which is the only proof of a correction (a collision is corrected by renumbering in the later week’s tool). Disposition every other finding: accept, with rationale and evidence: the gap stays in the package on purpose: the rationale says why it is acceptable and cites the evidence (an ID in the package, or the week whose record holds it); defer to a later week, with an owner: the gap is real and open (status: open); it is carried forward with the week it closes in and who closes it. Hard gates for each finding: a disposition: accept with rationale, or defer to a later week (to correct it, fix the week’s record and import its new export); a collision cannot be accepted; the rationale (a short one is enough: “Duplicate of REQ-STK-004”); “n/a” is not a rationale, and neither is “Fixed”, “Resolved” or “Withdrawn” while the finding is still found; a plan that names a Phase II week (“To be closed in Week 10”) is a rationale; for an accepted finding: the evidence the rationale rests on, an ID in the package or the week whose record holds it (“Week 2 record”); for a deferred finding: who closes it (a role or initials; “TBD” is not an owner); for a deferred finding: the week it closes in (Week 8 to 14). Advisory: this disposition was carried from an earlier week’s record: read it again and keep it only if it is still true; the rationale names a week that has passed: say what happened then; the owner is only a week: a week is a date, not an owner; name a person or role. Rule: every finding has a disposition that passes its gates (none silent). *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 11 ‘Coverage, findings and dispositions’; Session 1, slide 13 ‘Worked example: AeroLink’s package audit’.)*
4. **Write the executive summary and the lessons learned.** Six fields, each a gate: the recommendation: what you ask the reviewer to approve (one sentence); the evidence: at least one ID that exists in the package; the conditions: what must stay true for the recommendation to hold; the residual risk: at least one R ID that exists in the package; the decisions you need from the reviewer; the next roadmap increment: an INC ID after the baseline. Then at least one lesson learned: what the team would do differently, and why. Rules: at least one lesson learned, written; the AI usage summary for the package: at least 12 words, present even if no AI tool was used. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 6 ‘A baseline records what is open, with its history’; Session 1, slide 7 ‘The executive summary: six fields a decision maker reads first’.)*
5. **Prepare the review.** Name an owner for every package section, choose the key decision you defend and the thread you walk. Hard gates: every package section has an owner (initials are enough); the key decision you defend: your Week 3 trade decision or a Week 4 DEC; the thread you walk: a need whose trace reaches a system requirement and an architecture element. Advisories: the thread stops before an interface: choose a need whose element has an interface, or say why; no roadmap increment, gap or unknown touches the thread: the walk ends at the architecture. Build the briefing on the six-link argument: claim, evidence by ID, criteria and weights, conditions, residual risk, what would change it. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 12 ‘Worked example: one AeroLink thread, end to end’; Session 1, slide 16 ‘The Phase I review’s entry and exit criteria’; Session 1, slide 17 ‘Review roles: owners, reviewers, chair, recorder’; Session 2, slide 3 ‘The structure of an engineering argument’; Session 2, slide 4 ‘Defending the trade: AeroLink’s Year-2 decision’; Session 2, slide 5 ‘Defending a risk position and a roadmap’; Session 2, slide 6 ‘Briefing a mixed audience’.)*
6. **Review a peer package.** Read the sections of the assigned package that match your own role and write at least two review findings on the RID form: where, what is wrong against which criterion, severity, and a proposed fix. At least one should concern meaning or traceability, not style. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 15 ‘Prepare, conduct, complete’; Session 1, slide 18 ‘The closest professional analogy: the SRR’; Session 2, slide 7 ‘Review findings: the review item discrepancy (RID)’.)*
7. **Present and answer in the review.** Brief the summary, defend the key decision and walk the thread in the briefing time; in the questions, the owner of each section answers the findings on it. Timing for five teams: 4 min briefing and 4 min questions. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 2 ‘A defendable decision is traceable, and states its limits’; Session 2, slide 10 ‘Opening the review: criteria, roles and timing’.)*
8. **Close the review.** Enter every finding you received in the package tool with its disposition. Hard gates for each: where: an ID in the package or the title of a package section; what is wrong (one sentence); the criterion or source it breaks (a rule, a review criterion or a cited source); severity: major or minor; a disposition: accept, defer or reject; the response: what changes, or when; for a rejection, its evidence (an ID, or a reason in a phrase: “Out of scope”; a bare word such as “No” is not evidence); for an accepted or deferred RID: who makes the change (initials are enough). Advisory: a rejection that cites no ID: point the reviewer to the record, decision or computation that answers the finding. Rule: after the review: every RID received has a disposition that passes its gates. Export the closed package and upload it. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 8 ‘Answering findings: accept, defer or reject’; Session 2, slide 9 ‘Worked example: four RIDs against the AeroLink package’; Session 2, slide 12 ‘Closing the review: what happens next’.)*

## Presentation instructions

- **Length.** The class review block is 40 minutes shared equally: with five teams a 8-minute slot, 4 minutes of briefing and 4 of questions; with four teams 10 minutes, 6 and 4. The timekeeper stops the briefing at its time. A slot below 8 minutes is a faculty decision.
- **Content of the briefing.** The executive summary (recommendation, why, conditions, residual risk, decisions needed, next increment); one key decision defended with the six-link argument (claim, evidence by ID, criteria and weights, conditions, residual risk, what would change it); one thread walked through the package from a need to the architecture and on to an interface and the roadmap where it reaches them, naming each link; the open items that matter most to Week 8.
- **Who speaks.** Every member presents part of the briefing, and the owner of each package section answers the questions on it. Answer with IDs and links, not adjectives; say “I don’t know, it is recorded as an open item owned by…” when that is the truth.
- **Slides.** One message per slide, the IDs visible and the jargon removed; the decision and its conditions first, in operational terms; the evidence one step away, in the package, ready to open when asked. Upload the briefing with the package.

## Review instructions

- **Before the meeting (reviewers).** Read the assigned package from its index; each member reads the sections matching their own role. Write at least two RIDs each on the form, at least one about meaning or traceability, each with a location, the criterion it breaks, a severity and a proposed fix. Upload them before Session 2.
- **In the meeting.** The peer team raises its RIDs during the questions, major ones first; the recorder writes each RID raised and the owner’s first answer; the instructor chairs and decides. Reviewers ask; they do not argue the answer in the room.
- **After the meeting (owners).** Within 3 days the owning team dispositions every RID in the package tool: accept (the finding is right; the response says what changes, and an owner makes the change); defer (the finding is right but closes later; the response says when, and an owner carries it); reject (the finding is not a defect; the response gives the evidence (an ID, a recorded decision, a computation)). Accepted and deferred RIDs get an owner; deferred ones are carried into Week 8 as open items.

## Why each check blocks, or only advises

A hard gate stops an entry counting only where a sentence you can read in the lecture says the failure is a real defect; the last column names that slide and quotes the sentence. Anything that can occur in good work is an advisory, shown and never blocking. No check tests the form or length of a name.

A one-word answer counts wherever the lecture calls a word complete (“Duplicate”, “TL”, “R-005”), but an answer that says nothing (“n/a”, “none”, “?”, “-”, “later”, “TBD”) never counts where an answer is required: it records no reason, names no owner and answers no reviewer.

## Submission

- **Format.** Export two files from the package tool: team-NN-week7-package.md (the package index with its readiness report) and team-NN-week7-package.json (the package file). Add the briefing as a PDF or deck. Each file states the package’s readiness.
- **Where and when.** One member uploads the files to the LMS assignment “Week 7 Phase I package” at least 24 h before Session 2, and the closed package within 3 days of Session 2. Each member uploads their RIDs to the “Week 7 peer review” assignment before Session 2.
- **How it is graded.** The design-review presentation rubric on the Assessment page: six team criteria, two individual criteria, four levels each. Weights follow the course assessment table; the rubric is proposed, pending faculty approval.
- **AI use.** AI tools may help draft the executive summary wording, check the package for missing fields and rehearse questions; tag each AI-suggested entry AI-suggested-reviewed and say in the AI usage summary what the tool suggested and what you changed or rejected. The team owns every disposition, decision and answer.
- **Graduate task.** Graduate students also write a package trace-audit memo (one page, about 40 minutes; four criteria on four levels, 16 points): the coverage of each trace layer with the counting rule; every orphan and childless parent with its disposition, judged for whether it will still hold in Phase II; the one gap that most threatens the Week 8 import, traced to the model elements it would corrupt; and what the structural audit cannot see in your package, with one example found by reading.

## The package tool

The package tool reads your six weekly exports with each week’s own importer and builds the package: the index of every ID by the week that defines it, the typed links, the coverage of every trace layer and the findings, consistency across the weeks (stale exports, IDs no longer defined, wording drift, collisions where two elements share one ID, dispositions no longer true, open items an earlier week recorded and a later one dropped) and traceability (childless needs, orphan requirements, requirements not flowed down or not allocated, capabilities without a function, elements and externals without an interface, increments serving unknown IDs), with the open items of your Week 6 baseline review. Where an earlier week recorded a reason for a gap, the tool carries it as a starting disposition for you to confirm. Every disposition, summary field, review choice and review finding lists its hard gates, each marked pass or fails, and its advisories, which never block. One-word answers are accepted wherever the lecture calls a word complete; an answer that says nothing is not. The dispositions are three: corrected and re-imported (never typed: the finding disappears), accepted with a rationale that cites its evidence, or deferred to a Phase II week with an owner; a rationale that only claims “fixed”, “resolved” or “withdrawn” is refused while the finding is still found, and a collision can only be deferred. Teammates share the package file, and the tool asks before replacing work. Everything is saved in your browser only, so export after every working session.
