## Engineering method

1. **ConOps narrative.** Describe the existing situation the users want to change, the justification for the change and its restrictions, and the proposed system in the users’ language; record the operational environment (label and value), the user interactions and the operational constraints. State the scenario system of interest in one sentence: what is inside it decides who is an actor outside it. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 5 ‘What a ConOps is, and why it comes before requirements’; Session 1, slide 7 ‘What every ConOps must contain’; Session 1, slide 8 ‘Writing it well: the user’s language, environment, constraints’; Session 1, slide 9 ‘The AeroLink ConOps at a glance’.)*
2. **Scenarios on the course template.** Write each scenario on the course template (actors, trigger, preconditions, main flow, alternate flows, success end state, failure end state) against the stated scenario system of interest: exactly one primary actor, outside it; a trigger that is an event; at least 3 numbered steps, each with its actor; at least 1 alternate flow that branches from a step and rejoins a later step or ends; and both a success and an observable failure end state. Review each scenario with MITRE’s questions before it is final. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 10 ‘Use cases, actors and threads’; Session 1, slide 11 ‘Anatomy of a scenario: the course template’; Session 1, slide 12 ‘Which scenarios? Coverage and the off-nominal case’; Session 1, slide 13 ‘Reviewing a scenario’; Session 1, slide 14 ‘Worked example I: SCN-005 Weather Disruption, step by step’.)*
3. **Discovery log.** For every need, assumption or unknown a scenario exposes, log where it appeared (trigger, step or alternate) and send it on: a new need into the needs set (Part 5 revised), an assumption or an unknown into the uncertainty register, each with an owner. An unknown carries a numbered TBD or TBR token that requirements can cite. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 15 ‘Worked example II: what writing the scenario exposed’.)*
4. **Coverage check.** Build the needs × scenarios matrix: mark the scenarios (and steps) that exercise each need, and record a reason for every need no scenario exercises. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 16 ‘Worked example III: do the scenarios exercise the needs?’.)*
5. **Capabilities.** Derive capabilities from the needs: each says what the enterprise must be able to do, traces to at least one need, may refine a broader capability, and is linked to the scenarios that exercise it. Note the needs no capability covers yet. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 17 ‘What a capability is’; Session 1, slide 18 ‘The AeroLink capability tree’; Session 1, slide 19 ‘What the tree does not yet cover’.)*
6. **Requirements on the course record.** Write stakeholder requirements from needs and capabilities, and system requirements from stakeholder requirements and capabilities, each with the course record (ID; name; statement; type; measurable criterion or a TBD/TBR linked to an unknown; rationale; parent(s); verification method; owner). Every requirement must pass the hard gates: exactly one “shall”; a subject before “shall”; a parent that exists in the record; a type; a verification method; a verification criterion; a value or a TBD/TBR on every performance requirement; every TBD/TBR linked to an unknown with an owner; a rationale that is not a restatement; no unmeasurable word (easy to use, quickly, adequate, safe, quiet, maximize …) unless a bounded value or a TBD/TBR defines it. The advisories (a vague word (quickly, adequate, user-friendly …); “maximize” or “minimize”; “shall not”; a single-point value with no bound or range; wording that may name a design (using, via, a product or rating); “and” or “or” joining two actions; a method that may not fit the type (for example a performance value checked by inspection)) are signs of a likely defect that a good requirement can also show: rewrite or keep each one knowingly. Cover at least 3 of the four types (functional, performance, interface, constraint); every requirement names a method (inspection, analysis, demonstration, test). *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 20 ‘Two levels: stakeholder and system requirements’; Session 1, slide 21 ‘One chain, end to end: NEED-004 to REQ-SYS-001’; Session 2, slide 3 ‘Anatomy of a requirement and its attributes’; Session 2, slide 4 ‘Characteristics of good requirements’; Session 2, slide 5 ‘Types and categories: organizing a set so gaps show’; Session 2, slide 6 ‘Level, white space and derived requirements’; Session 2, slide 7 ‘Negatives, ranges, thresholds and TBDs’; Session 2, slide 8 ‘A verification method for every requirement’; Session 2, slide 9 ‘Worked example IV: two chains and the reasoning in the rationale’; Session 2, slide 10 ‘Worked example V: a Week 1 correction reaches the requirements’; Session 2, slide 11 ‘Worked example VI: critique and rewrite’.)*
7. **Trace audit, forward and backward.** Trace forward from every need to a stakeholder requirement and from every stakeholder requirement to a system requirement, and backward from every requirement to a parent that exists. A need counts as covered only when a stakeholder requirement names it as a parent; a trace through a shared capability does not cover it. List the orphans and fix them; give every childless need and childless stakeholder requirement, and every need a requirement covers only in part, one disposition: write the requirement, link the real parent, or record why not (open item: what is missing, who closes it and the target week; or no child needed: why no lower-level requirement will carry it, for example because it is verified at service level). Never link a requirement that does not carry the need only to make the matrix look complete. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 12 ‘Bidirectional traceability: orphans and childless parents’; Session 2, slide 13 ‘Worked example VII: auditing the AeroLink trace’.)*
8. **Measures.** Define measures of effectiveness (each measures a need, a capability or the mission or a goal) and measures of performance (each supports a measure of effectiveness and measures a requirement), each with a definition, a unit, a threshold and its data source. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 15 ‘Measures of effectiveness and measures of performance’; Session 2, slide 16 ‘Worked example VIII: the AeroLink measures’.)*

## The lab tool

The lab tool imports your team’s Week 1 model-inputs file (or the Week 1 record saved in the same browser) and adds Parts 7–11. Each part shows its checks as you type, the same checks the instructor applies first. For every requirement it lists the hard gates (exactly one “shall”; a subject before “shall”; a parent that exists in the record; a type; a verification method; a verification criterion; a value or a TBD/TBR on every performance requirement; every TBD/TBR linked to an unknown with an owner; a rationale that is not a restatement; no unmeasurable word (easy to use, quickly, adequate, safe, quiet, maximize …) unless a bounded value or a TBD/TBR defines it), each marked pass or fails, and the advisories, labeled advisory, which never stop a requirement counting. Only requirements that pass every hard gate count toward the minimums. One member can enter the record and export the JSON file; teammates import it to continue the same record. Everything is saved in your browser only, so export after every working session.
