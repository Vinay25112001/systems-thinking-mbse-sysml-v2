Week 9 — Needs / Requirements. AeroLink is the instructor’s FICTIONAL worked example; this model is of your team’s own enterprise.

## Purpose

Model your team’s own needs and requirements, never AeroLink, in the SysML v2 model your team built in Week 8. Every Week 1 need becomes a requirement usage that names its stakeholders and frames the concerns it answers; every Week 2 requirement becomes a requirement usage with its ID, its statement, a subject and, where it can be written, a required constraint; every parent link becomes a derivation. Then audit the trace from needs to requirements, review the requirements with an AI tool and decide every finding yourself, and run the model through the course-designated checker until it reports no errors. Model v0.2 is what Weeks 10 to 13 connect functions, design elements, verification cases and analyses to.

## Engineering method

1. **Needs.** Each Week 1 need becomes a requirement usage in Needs: its ID as a quoted short name, its statement, condition and rationale as doc, the enterprise as subject, a stakeholder parameter typed by the Week 8 part definition of each stakeholder, and a frame for each concern it answers, and only those. Needs added in Week 2 have no stakeholders or concerns in the export: enter them in the tool, or record why none. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 13 ‘Concerns and framing’; Session 1, slide 14 ‘AeroLink worked example I: the Needs package’.)*
2. **Mission and objectives.** The mission and each objective become requirement usages with the enterprise as subject; each need has a #serves dependency, from the need to the objective it serves. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 19 ‘Dependencies, refinement and the direction rule’.)*
3. **Stakeholder requirements.** Each stakeholder requirement has the enterprise as subject. Name the measured feature of the enterprise, the comparison and the limit with its unit, from the Week 2 criterion; the tool adds the feature to the enterprise and writes the limit and the required constraint. A requirement left informal needs a disposition saying why. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 10 ‘Doc alone is not enough: the required constraint’; Session 2, slide 5 ‘Worked example III: anatomy of a stakeholder requirement’.)*
4. **System requirements.** Choose each system requirement’s subject first: the element its shall is about, an existing part definition of your model, or a named placeholder in Physical or Logical that Weeks 10 and 11 complete. Then the feature, comparison and limit. Mark two or more requirements of the same quantity and comparison as sharing a definition, or record why none share a form. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 8 ‘The subject: what the requirement is about’; Session 1, slide 16 ‘Worked example II: three latency requirements, one definition’.)*
5. **TBDs and TBRs.** Keep the constraint of every requirement with a TBD or TBR. Leave a TBD limit empty: the tool records status tbd and the owner from your Week 2 register (enter the owner if none is linked). A TBR keeps its value and is recorded as tbc. Where a satisfier is known, consider binding the limit to its configured value by hand, as the worked example shows. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 6 ‘Worked example IV: a limit that is still to be decided’.)*
6. **Assumptions.** Write an assumption only when the condition bounds where the requirement applies and the required quantity depends on it; otherwise keep the condition in the feature’s name and the doc. Justify each assumption in one sentence in the trace-audit note. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 11 ‘Assumptions: when a requirement applies’; Session 2, slide 7 ‘Worked example V: “in still air”: gate or reference condition?’.)*
7. **Derivations and the trace audit.** The tool writes one derivation for each parent link to a need or a requirement, the parent as the original end; links to capabilities wait for Week 10. Read the trace matrix and give every trace finding a disposition: an open item with an owner and a week, or no child needed with the reason. A disposition cannot claim a fix: correct the record, export it and import it again. Tick every need your Week 2 audit recorded as only partly covered and record its open item (the missing part, an owner, a week): the need then reads partial, not covered, in the matrix and the export. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 18 ‘Derivation: the library connection’; Session 2, slide 9 ‘The trace matrix, read from the model’.)*
8. **AI-assisted quality review.** Export the trace-matrix file, ask an AI tool for findings in four categories (ambiguity, duplication, conflict, missing information), each tied to a requirement ID and a quoted phrase, and record every finding in the tool’s AI review log with your decision, the reason, and for an accepted one the change you made. At least 8 findings (a team of three: 6). Re-read every changed requirement against its need. *(Taught in: Session 2, slide 13 ‘The method: AI findings are candidates to disposition’; Session 2, slide 14 ‘Demonstrated on AeroLink: a seeded review set’.)*
9. **Checker to zero errors.** Run model v0.2 through the course-designated checker and correct it until the checker reports no errors; explain every warning in the log. A PASS shows the file parsed, every name resolved and no rule fired; it does not show that a requirement is the right one, that its subject is the right element, or that any design meets it. *(Taught in: Session 1, slide 22 ‘What the checker checks, and what it cannot’; Session 2, slide 10 ‘Common errors in modeled requirements’.)*

## Trace-audit finding classes (course convention)

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Defect (left out of the file) | Writing it would refer to something that is not in your model, so the checker would fail. It is left out of the generated file and listed; correct the Week 1, Week 2 or Week 8 file and import again. |
| Gap (fix it in the tool, or record why not) | The element is generated, but something the course expects is missing: a stakeholder, a framed concern, a formal constraint, a subject, an owner. Fix it here where the tool allows; otherwise write a disposition. |
| Trace finding (needs a disposition) | A need with no stakeholder requirement, a stakeholder requirement with no system requirement, or a requirement with no parent. Record what the team will do and why: an open item with an owner, or no child needed with the reason. |
| Information | Recorded for the trace matrix (for example, a parent capability that Week 10 models). No action this week. |

## The lab tool

The lab tool reads three files: your Week 8 model v0.1 (.sysml), your Week 1 model inputs and your Week 2 model inputs (.json). It refuses AeroLink files, files of another team and files whose packages are missing, each with the reason. For every requirement you choose the subject and enter the measured feature, the comparison, the limit and its unit; for every need you can correct its stakeholders and concerns. The tool writes model v0.2: it fills the Needs and Requirements packages, adds the measured features to the subjects and the placeholder subjects you name, and writes the derivations, groups and metadata. It computes the trace matrix and the trace audit, keeps the AI review log and shows a readiness list with the reason for every item still open. Only a real defect keeps an item out of the model (a reference to a stakeholder, concern or parent that is not in your files would not resolve). Its checks are course acceptance checks: whether the model is valid is decided by the course-designated checker, and a checker PASS does not show that a requirement is the right one. A model v0.2 exported from the tool can be imported again; the tool replaces what it generated, so keep hand edits in a separate file until the last export. Everything is saved in your browser only: export after every working session, and share the workspace file with teammates.
