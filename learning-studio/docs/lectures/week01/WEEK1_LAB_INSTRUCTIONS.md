Week 1 — Team Formation / Needs / Identifying Stakeholders. AeroLink is the instructor’s FICTIONAL worked example; this record is about your team’s own enterprise.

## Purpose

Apply the Week 1 method to your team’s own enterprise, never to AeroLink: form the team, propose the enterprise and its system of interest for approval, draw its context, find and classify its stakeholders, and write and audit its first needs. The result, the Week 1 Team Record, is the first part of the Systems Thinking Engineering Package (Week 7), and its IDs become the keys of your SysML v2 model in Phase II.

## Objectives

- **B1.1** Form a project team with a charter that assigns roles, decision rules, communication norms and ID ownership
- **B1.3** Propose an enterprise and its system of interest, with a boundary and a context, for instructor approval
- **B1.4** Identify and classify the stakeholders of a system of interest, with their specific concerns
- **B1.5** Write well-formed stakeholder need statements traced to stakeholders, and tell needs from requirements
- **B1.7** Evaluate a set of needs for coverage, conflict and priority (the coverage audit) *(proposed, pending faculty approval)*

## Engineering method

1. **Charter.** In class: assign the lead roles to different members, agree decision rules including a tie-break, and choose the team’s lead candidate enterprise. By the end of day 1: set communication norms and give each member owned ID prefixes, with one owner each for ENT, PRT, EXT, STK, CON, NEED, REL (Architecture usually holds ENT, PRT, EXT and REL). *(Taught in: Session 1, team charter; Session 2, lab launch.)*
2. **Enterprise and system of interest.** Name the enterprise and its domain; state the problem statement in one sentence; state the mission (MGO-001) in one sentence, a purpose and not a product, and break it into goals (MGO-002 …); state the system of interest inside the enterprise in one sentence; list the elements inside the boundary of the system of interest, each with a PRT ID (a PRT is an element inside the boundary of the system of interest; in Week 8 each becomes a part of your system); and argue why it suits a 14-week project. *(Taught in: Session 1, system concepts and Business or Mission Analysis.)*
3. **Context.** Draw the boundary, then the immediate environment: each external system or actor, what crosses the boundary (people, materials, energy, information, approvals) and its direction. *(Taught in: Session 1, boundary and context.)*
4. **Stakeholder identification.** Run all three methods: the life-cycle sweep, the context (who stands behind each external system) and the role classes. Check approving authorities explicitly; a register without one is incomplete. *(Taught in: Session 1, identifying stakeholders.)*
5. **Classification and concerns.** For each stakeholder record the role, the life-cycle stage, internal or external, whether it is an approving authority, one or more specific concerns, and influence from 1 to 5 with a reason. *(Taught in: Session 1, classifying; Session 2, concerns.)*
6. **Relationships.** Record how stakeholders relate as REL rows (from, to, type, note): at least 3, including at least 1 conflict between two stakeholders’ concerns (“conflicts with”). Required, as the lecture sets it (faculty decision recorded; scoring under RB-1.4 is proposed). *(Taught in: Session 2, the register and conflict.)*
7. **Needs.** Write each need with the six-step method (start from the concern, ask why, strip design and requirement wording, attribute, state the condition, record and check), preferably in the recommended form “The [stakeholders] need the [system] to …”, which names your system of interest and keeps the stakeholder’s view. Another phrasing is accepted when it names the stakeholder and the outcome and is neither a requirement nor a solution. Check it against the layered characteristics and the six minimum checks (stated as a need, not a requirement; no design; attributed to a registered stakeholder; traced to its concern; a condition; a rationale); trace it to the concern it answers and to the mission or a goal; give it a priority in MITRE’s categories (mandatory, critical, desirable or optional, as the lecture defines them). *(Taught in: Session 2, needs and the worked example.)*
8. **Coverage audit.** Build the stakeholder-by-need matrix. For every stakeholder without a need, either add a need or record why its concern is not a need on the system of interest; check that every approving authority is covered and look for conflicts between needs. *(Taught in: Session 2, worked example III.)*

## Submission

- **Format.** Export two files from the Team enterprise view: team-NN-week1-record.md (the six parts as headed Markdown, ready to read) and team-NN-week1-model-inputs.json (the same data with IDs and the SysML v2 element each item will seed). - **Where and when.** One team member uploads both files to the “Week 1 Team Record” assignment in the course learning management system before the first session of Week 2. The proposal carries the instructor’s decision from the Session 2 checkpoint: “approved” with its date, or, if the instructor has not yet decided, “submitted for approval”. A record submitted for approval is labeled as such and is not shown as ready; the instructor’s approval is required before the Week 2 submission.
- **How it is graded.** Formative: you receive feedback, and the record does not count toward the course grade this week. The completeness checks come first; the rubric rows below are then applied. The charter and the instructor’s decision are completeness checks only.
- **AI use.** AI tools may be used to brainstorm stakeholders and to critique draft needs. Every AI-suggested stakeholder or need must be tagged AI-suggested-reviewed, and the AI usage statement must say what was rejected.

## The lab tool

The Team enterprise view is the lab tool. Each part shows its checks as you type, the same checks the instructor applies first: that each need is a sentence naming a registered stakeholder and an outcome, not a requirement (“shall”) or a named solution (a need in another phrasing than “The [stakeholders] need the [system] to …” is accepted, with a note recommending that form), the six minimum checks, the trace of every need to its concern and to the mission or a goal, and the relationship rows. One member can enter the record and export the model inputs; teammates import that JSON file to see and continue the same record. Everything is saved in your browser only, so export after every working session.

The Week 1 model inputs are the engineering data your SysML v2 model will need in Phase II, captured now with stable IDs and sources. They are not a separate assignment: the Team enterprise view exports them, as JSON, from the same entries as your Team Record. The table shows what each item later seeds in SysML v2; you will learn those elements from Week 8.

| Week 1 item | Captured as | Seeds in SysML v2 (Phase II reading) |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise and system of interest (ENT-001) | Name, problem statement, system-of-interest statement | `part def`: the system of interest as a part definition (R70 p.9); `documentation (doc)`: the purpose, as documentation owned by that definition (R70 p.8) |
| Mission and goals (MGO) | The mission MGO-001 and each goal MGO-002 …, with its statement | `documentation (doc)`: the mission and goals as documentation the needs trace to (course working mapping; Week 3 develops them) (R70 p.8) |

| External systems and actors (EXT) | ID, name, kind (system or actor) | `part (usage) in a context`: external systems as part usages beside the system of interest in a context part (course working mapping) (R70 p.9); `actor`: an external role in a use case: a parameter representing a role played by an entity external to the subject (R76 p.100) |
| What crosses the boundary, and its direction | Exchange and direction per EXT | `item def`: what flows (people, materials, energy, information) (R70 p.17); `port def with in / out features`: the direction of each flow (R71 p.20) |
| Stakeholders (STK) | ID, name, classification, level of interest with a reason (required); influence and support (optional) | `part def + stakeholder`: a part definition for each stakeholder, used as the stakeholder parameter of a concern or requirement (R77 p.115) |
| Relationships between stakeholders (REL) | ID, from and to (STK IDs), type, note; at least one conflict | `connection or dependency`: a relationship between two stakeholder parts (course working mapping, settled in Week 8) |
| Concerns (CON) | One or more specific concerns per stakeholder, each with its own CON ID and its stakeholder | `concern`: a concern with doc, subject and stakeholder: a specific topic one or more stakeholders desire to be addressed (R77 p.115) |
| Needs (NEED) | ID, name, statement, condition, rationale, priority, source stakeholders, the concerns it answers, the goal it serves (MGO), the team’s self-check | `requirement def / requirement`: short name <'NEED-001'>, the statement as doc, the system of interest as subject, the source as a stakeholder parameter (course working mapping: SysML v2 has no separate “need” element) (R74 p.86–87); `frame`: a requirement may frame the concerns it addresses: the need’s CON links (R77 p.116) |
